# La Union fish trade: a field guide for MERMAID's marketplace

**The short version for product:** La Union's fish trade is not a simple fisher-to-vendor dyad — it is a three-layer chain (**fisher → comprador/financier → byahero → tindera**) held together by credit, not price. Any marketplace feature that tries to disintermediate by routing a fisher's catch directly to a palengke stall will collide with an interest-free-loan-for-exclusive-supply contract that has already been signed, often years before the app was installed. The highest-leverage design move is therefore **not** fisher-to-vendor matching; it is **byahero-to-tindera matching** overlaid on transparent benchmark prices, anchored at **Damortis in Sto. Tomas** (the province's dominant landing site and Lingayen Gulf's main La-Union-side port), and deployed in **Ilocano-first UX with SMS fallback** because ~30–50% of small-scale fishers still use feature phones and cell coverage in outer coastal barangays degrades during typhoons exactly when the app matters most. The rest of this report gives the evidence and the concrete implications.

---

## 1. The seven coastal palengkes and how they operate

Every main fish market in La Union's seven coastal LGUs is **LGU-managed**, not private — each has a Market Code ordinance and a municipal economic-enterprise or market-operations unit. San Fernando City is the most institutionally formalized, with a dedicated **City Market Operations Division** headed by Market Supervisor IV Lorena S. Peridas (contact: 072-619-4908, eem@sanfernandocity.gov.ph), overseeing both the Old and New Public Markets. The city-market complex is the largest wet market in the province and has a separately renovated "Round Market" section.

The other six markets form a rough north-to-south chain along MacArthur Highway / Manila North Road: **Bauang Commercial Center** (two-storey, wet section at rear; **Thursdays and Sundays are special market days** with overflow street stalls — the only LGU in scope with publicly documented market days); **Caba Municipal Market** (newly rebuilt, small — Caba is a 4th-class LGU of ~23,000); **Aringay Public Market** (OTOP product: bangus); **Agoo Public Market** (dedicated fish section, with a **new multi-species hatchery and aquaculture facility** inaugurated January 16, 2025, in Sta. Rita Central); **Rosario Public Market** in Brgy. Subusub (9,781 m², six buildings plus five ambulant structures plus two new roadside buildings, still expanding); and the **Santo Tomas Public Market in Brgy. Damortis** — which is co-located with the Damortis Fish Port / ice plant / BFAR Mariculture Park and fronts the famous "Tabo-an of the North" / "Daing Capital of La Union" strip of dried-fish stalls along the National Highway.

**Operating hours are almost universally undocumented online** for all seven LGUs. The typical Philippine rhythm applies: **wholesale/bagsakan 3–5 AM**, **retail 5 AM – 6/7 PM**, peak freshness first two hours. For MERMAID this means **in-app "market open" filters must be set manually by each LGU market supervisor** during onboarding — there is no authoritative online dataset to scrape. Market supervisors are the single best institutional entry point per municipality. San Fernando City's office is the obvious flagship.

**Recent modernization is uneven but active.** Bacnotan (adjacent, out of scope) opened Phase III with the province's first market-building elevator in March 2025. Caba and Rosario have rebuilt or are rebuilding. San Fernando acknowledges its market is "bursting at the seams." **SM City La Union opened October 17, 2025**, in Brgy. Biday (San Fernando) with an SM Supermarket; it does not displace palengke vendors but adds competitive retail for middle-class consumers.

## 2. Who fishes, who sells, and the channel that actually moves the fish

La Union has an active small-scale fisher base across all six southern coastal LGUs (Bauang, Caba, Aringay, Agoo, Sto. Tomas, Rosario) — BFAR's 2018 fiberglass-boat distribution placed 36 units across exactly these six towns. In 2023, La Union's marine municipal fisheries landed **3,385 MT** with top species sapsap, bisugo, tambakol, alimasag, gulyasan; Q1 2025 surged to 2,824 MT driven by bangus (524 MT, 18.5%).

**The sourcing channel that dominates is not "tindera drives to the beach."** Based on Rosales et al.'s ECOFISH value chain analysis of Lingayen Gulf (2017, *Marine Policy* 83:11–21) and Peralta-Milan's 2020 PhD on Lingayen Gulf reef-seafood markets, the chain is:

**Fisher → Comprador/Financier (first buyer at landing, who bankrolled the trip) → Byahero/Tagabiyahe (transports by tricycle, multicab, or L300 van) → Tindera (stall vendor) → Consumer.**

Direct fisher-to-tindera happens only in three narrow cases: (a) the tindera is the fisher's wife or close kin, which is the dominant pattern in **Damortis daing households** where "the men would give their catch to their wives who are expert in dried fish processing"; (b) very small-volume subsistence surplus sold door-to-door; (c) longtime personal suki bonds at very small landing beaches in Bauang, Caba, San Juan. By volume, the **byahero is the real node** between beach and market.

In Lingayen Gulf's open landings the catch is also price-discovered by **bulungan** (whispered auction) and **consignacion** (sell-on-commission with pay-after-sale), systems identical to those documented at Madridejos (Visayan Sea) and Matain (Zambales). **Design implication:** a marketplace app whose mental model is "Uber for fish, fisher lists → vendor buys" will miss 80% of actual transactions. The right primitive is **byahero listings of consolidated loads, priced against a transparent benchmark, visible to tinderas across multiple palengkes simultaneously**.

## 3. Damortis is the supply node, but most fish does not leave La Union fresh

Damortis in Sto. Tomas is **the main Lingayen Gulf landing on the La Union side** (FAO confirms), with a municipal wharf, ice plant, BFAR mariculture park, and adjoining Institute of Fisheries under DMMMSU. Flows out of Damortis rank roughly as: **(1) local La Union palengkes** (Sto. Tomas itself, Rosario, Agoo, Aringay, Bauang, San Fernando — 5–40 km hops by tricycle or van); **(2) dried-fish processing absorbed within Damortis barangay** — a very large share of sapsap, espada, dilis, padas, and pusit never leaves the barangay fresh, instead becoming the daing strip's inventory; **(3) Baguio City's Magsaysay Fish Market** by truck up Kennon/Marcos/Aspiras highways (1.5–2 hrs); **(4) Dagupan City** (especially bangus) for cross-gulf consolidation; and **(5) Manila/Navotas — mostly in the reverse direction.** BFAR's January 2025 NCPMR confirms frozen galunggong **in La Union was sourced from Malabon, Navotas, Urdaneta, and Dagupan,** not the other way around. Damortis is a net **exporter of premium cage products (large bangus, lapu-lapu, pompano, sugpo) and a net importer of mass-market frozen pelagics**.

BFAR Region 1 has built **9 Community Fish Landing Centers (CFLCs) in La Union alone** (out of 44 in Region 1), including a well-documented one at Balaoan's Brgy. Paraoir (inaugurated February 2019). **Operational reality check:** NAPC and BFAR themselves acknowledged in early 2024 that many CFLCs nationwide are under-utilized or non-operational pending LGU capitalization and ordinances. For MERMAID this is both risk and opportunity — the physical infrastructure exists as onboarding touchpoints, but the app cannot assume CFLCs are fully functional wholesale markets.

## 4. What's on the stall is not what was caught

This is a surprising finding with direct product-catalog implications: **the species that dominate La Union's capture fisheries (sapsap, pusit, tunsoy, dilis, alumahan, bisugo) do not dominate wet market retail.** BFAR Region 1's weekly stall-composition monitoring shows the order is consistently **bangus (~30–37% of stalls), tilapia (~16–18%), galunggong (~14–15%), then everything else (~31–33%)**. In 2024, La Union's aquaculture production (3,163 MT of bangus alone) exceeded its municipal marine catch — but most of that La Union bangus actually moves to Dagupan consignación for regional distribution, while La Union palengkes **import bangus from Dagupan, Bolinao, Sual, and Anda** (Pangasinan's larger operations). The retail mix at a typical La Union stall roughly breaks down as ~30–40% local capture (Lingayen Gulf small pelagics and demersals), ~35–45% Pangasinan aquaculture (bangus dominant), ~10–15% freshwater tilapia from Pampanga/Batangas/Isabela, and ~10–15% imported-frozen or long-distance galunggong.

**Price anchors (2024–2025, BFAR Region 1 and NCPMR):** bangus medium ₱140–230, tilapia chilled ₱120–180, local galunggong ₱140–260 (up to ₱400 during Palawan's Nov 1–Jan 31 closed season), sapsap ₱280–450, bisugo ₱240–400, alumahan ₱300–450, pusit ₱350–500, lapu-lapu ₱400–600. The **Tier 1 MERMAID species picker** should therefore lead with bangus, tilapia, galunggong (with a local-vs-imported toggle), sapsap, bisugo, alumahan, pusit — and provide seasonality flags for **Amihan (Nov–Feb)** when rough seas cut marine landings, **Holy Week** demand spikes, and the **Palawan closed season** that disrupts galunggong supply.

## 5. Where the money leaks: markup and middleman layers

The supply chain has **two to three middleman layers** between fisher and consumer. Reconstructed markups are roughly: **bangus** ₱120–150 farm-gate → ₱160–200 La Union retail → ₱180–240 NCR (30–50% spread); **local fresh galunggong** ₱100–150 at landing → ₱200–280 retail (roughly 2×); **sapsap** ₱120–180 landing → ₱280–450 retail (2–2.5×). The **binding markup is typically Layer 1 — the comprador's purchase at the beach** — because the comprador holds both the pre-paid loan and the ice. Shortening only the byahero-to-tindera leg yields modest consumer savings; the real fisher income gain requires disturbing the Layer-1 price, which is politically the hardest.

## 6. The suki-comprador lock is a credit contract, not a relationship

This is the single most important finding for product strategy. The comprador system (ethnographically documented by Mangahas 2004 for Samal Island and confirmed by Rosales et al. 2017 for Lingayen Gulf) is **a multi-stranded credit relationship, not a transaction preference**. The comprador advances cash for fuel, ice, gear, rice, school fees, medical bills, and baptisms — often also running the barangay sari-sari store, which layers grocery credit on top of fishing credit. In return, the fisher sells exclusively to that comprador at a price typically 10–30% below open-beach rates. The loan is nominally interest-free; **the price discount is the interest**.

Layered on this is the **"5-6" informal lending market** (borrow ₱5, repay ₱6 → ~20% per cycle), which is documented for La Union fisherfolk specifically: TIMEK-La Union chair George Cacayuran told Inquirer that rising fuel costs force fishers into debt that trader underpricing then deepens; Lourdes Doctolero of Agoo told Global Press Journal her family has "always been in debt to afford fishing capital." Rep. Tony Roman's House Bill 4857 (Fisheries Asset Collateralization Act) was introduced explicitly to break fisherfolk 5-6 dependence.

**Implication for MERMAID, in one sentence:** a marketplace that offers a better price without replacing the liquidity/credit function will be rejected, because fishers cannot afford to lose their lender of last resort. The app must either (a) integrate with a cooperative-backed or Landbank-linked cash-advance-against-catch product, or (b) design with compradors as a verified tier, letting transparent prices compress their markup without severing the credit bond. Option (b) is the faster, less capital-intensive path.

## 7. Women run the retail, processing, and much of the household books

The Philippine pattern — men fish, women vend/process/manage money — is strongly confirmed for La Union. **Damortis daing stalls are explicitly documented as wife-run**, turning husbands' catches into dried sapsap, danggit, espada, turay, pusit, and dilis. Sto. Tomas's own municipal profile (2022) registered 125 fisherfolk — 90 male, 35 female — but this under-counts women's post-harvest labor, a well-known artifact of narrow fisheries definitions (Torell et al. 2020).

BFAR's **Gender and Development Project** has been active in Region 1 since 2004, training Rural Improvement Clubs in milkfish deboning, smoking, packaging, and bookkeeping — with Sto. Tomas La Union among the named pilot municipalities. BFAR's national **AHON program** explicitly pairs boats for men with **chest freezers for women fisherfolk** — directly relevant cold-chain hardware for MERMAID integration. In April 2022, DMMMSU's PEACE Project trained **10 women from Brgy. Narvacan, Sto. Tomas** in Gracilaria/tamsaw seaweed processing. **Design implication:** MERMAID's target user pair (mangngalap + tindera) is frequently **the same household**; a shared-phone scenario is realistic, and features like separate-login-same-device, simple transaction logs, and mobile-money integration (women disproportionately manage household cash flow) are high-value.

## 8. Named organizations and programs to partner with

The cooperative landscape is thin but identifiable. **Damortis Fisherfolks Cooperative** (Rosario, president Arnulfo Eslao Jr.) received a PHP 38M 62-footer steel-hulled ring-net vessel from BFAR in December 2022 — the province's highest-profile fisher org. **Magnificent Fisherfolks and Payao Association** (Ilocanos Sur, San Fernando City), **Sta. Rita Fishermen's Association** (Agoo), and **Sulong Libtong / Abante Cambaly / Imelda kay Ganda / Go Cares FAs** appear in BFAR SAAD Phase 2 distribution lists. **TIMEK La Union** (Timek ken Namnama Dagiti Babassit a Mangngalap ti La Union), chaired by George Cacayuran and based in Agoo, is the most visible small-scale-fisher advocacy organization in the province — worth direct engagement, though note that state/security sources have labeled it a "communist front," a characterization disputed by human-rights organizations and not supported by its public fisheries-policy advocacy record.

The **World Bank-funded FishCoRe Project** (~PHP 11.4B, 7 years) is active in La Union with livelihood subprojects in Sto. Tomas, San Fernando, and Bauang; 14 fisherfolk orgs from CAR and Region 1 received Business Management + Values Formation training in San Juan in May 2025. The **CDA–BFAR Region 1 MOA** (March 2021) is formalizing fisher associations into cooperatives; in June 2022, 30 associations from Sto. Tomas and 22 from Bacnotan entered pre-registration. **BFAR Region 1 Post-Harvest & Marketing Division** under OIC **Mea Baldonado** is the correct institutional counterpart for CFLC and marketing-program coordination.

## 9. Connectivity, devices, and digital tools already in play

La Union's connectivity is **above the national rural average but below urban NCR**. Globe and Smart dominate; DITO is limited to the San Fernando–Bauang corridor. The **DICT Regional Office 1 is headquartered in Bauang**, and DICT's Free WiFi for All (via Converge, 2023) serves four municipal sites in La Union — LGU centers only, not fishing barangays. 4G is solid along MacArthur Highway; 5G is emerging only in San Fernando/Bauang/San Juan tourist zones. Signal degrades in outer coastal barangays (interior sitios, seawall-lined stretches) and during typhoons — **Super Typhoon Uwan in November 2025 put Signal #4 over Bauang, Caba, Aringay, Agoo, Sto. Tomas, Rosario**, destroying boats in Sto. Tomas and Agoo and taking out power and cell towers, which is precisely when fisher safety features matter most.

Among fisherfolk themselves, NFRDI/Globe field reports state plainly: "hindi naman lahat nagmamay-ari ng smartphones… may mga small-scale na fisherfolk na analog phones lang ang gamit." Nationally, DICT's 2019 NICTHS found only **36% of rural Filipinos used the internet** in the prior 3 months and **11% of rural households had home internet**. Translating to La Union fishers: assume **~50–70% have smartphone access (often family-shared)** and **30–50% are on feature phones** — which means **SMS fallback is not a nice-to-have, it is table stakes**. This was exactly the logic behind BFAR/NFRDI/Globe's **ISDApp** (launched Sariaya 2021, Dingalan 2021), which uses a Community Controller model — one smartphone-owning leader broadcasts weather alerts via free SMS to fisher feature phones. **No ISDApp deployment exists in La Union yet** — a clean opportunity for MERMAID to either integrate or adopt the pattern.

The adjacent digital toolkit: **FishR** (BFAR municipal fisherfolk registry, 2.3M registered nationally) and **BoatR** (vessel registration) are LGU-facing databases, not consumer apps — useful as onboarding credentials. **Mayani** is the most relevant agri-fisheries marketplace precedent (144,000 farmers/fishers, GCash credit partnership since November 2023, $1.7M seed from AgFunder); worth direct study and possible partnership rather than duplication. **Karagatan Patrol** (Oceana + LMP) is enforcement-facing only. **GCash dominates e-wallet adoption** (~94M users) and is actively onboarding wet-market vendors through **Paleng-QR Ph Plus** (BSP + DILG + GCash), with successful rollouts in Pasig Mega Market and Project 4 QC (87% of vendors accept GCash QR). Assume GCash + Maya integration is near-mandatory for MERMAID's payment layer. **Facebook Messenger is the de facto internet** for rural Filipinos — 69% eligible-audience penetration — so a Messenger-bot or FB Page complement to the app may have wider reach than the app itself.

## 10. Ilocano-first, with a Pangasinan nod in the south

La Union speaks the **Abagatan (Southern) dialect of Iloco**, officially recognized via Provincial Ordinance 026-2012. **Sto. Tomas and Rosario** — the two southernmost towns, where Damortis sits — are historically bilingual Iloco/Pangasinan because Sto. Tomas was carved from Mangaldan, Pangasinan in 1764; older vendors at Damortis may still code-switch to Pangasinan. Filipino/Tagalog is universally understood but **Ilocano is the rapport language** — using it signals insider status and unlocks better prices and fresher picks.

A working glossary for MERMAID's UI localization:

| Function | Ilocano term |
|---|---|
| Fisher | **mangngalap** (root *kalap* = to fish) |
| To fish | **agkalap / panagkalap** |
| Fish (general) | **ikan** or **lames** |
| Market | **tiendaan** (palengke also understood) |
| To sell / seller | **aglako / aglaklako** |
| To buy | **gumatang / manggatang** |
| Price, payment | **bayad**; "Mano ti bayad na?" = How much? |
| Discount | **tawar**; "Adda tawar na?" |
| Expensive / cheap | **nangina / nalaka** |
| Fresh | **baro** (new); "baro a lames" |
| Boat | **bangka** (common) / **bilog** (classical) |
| Net | **iket** |
| Dried fish | **daing** (generic) |
| Anchovy (dilis) | **monamon / munamon** (classic Ilocos term) |
| Fish-paste | **bugguong** (bugguong munamon = anchovy paste) |
| Goby fry (Ilocos-specific) | **ipon** — seasonal Sep–Feb, ₱400–700/kg |
| Round scad | **galunggong** or **tamodios** |
| Thank you / good morning | **Agyamanak / Naimbag nga bigat, Apo!** |

For Sto. Tomas/Rosario, consider a Pangasinan greeting toggle (*Maabig ya kabuasan*) as a trust signal, not a full UI language. Voice/audio prompts in Ilocano would dramatically improve adoption among older, lower-literacy fishers.

## Ten concrete UX decisions this research points to

The research converges on a coherent set of design calls. **Anchor the pilot at Damortis** — it has the volume, the ice plant, the CFLC ecosystem, the byahero traffic to Baguio and Dagupan, and the densest cluster of tinderas and daing processors in La Union. **Lead the supply-side UX with byaheros, not fishers**, because byaheros are smartphone-native, mobile, already price-shopping, and unbound by comprador loans; fisher registration should be a secondary, cooperative-mediated onboarding flow. **Treat compradors as a feature, not a bug** — let them list catch as a "verified first-buyer" tier, using MERMAID's price transparency to compress their markup without severing the credit function. **Build offline-first with SMS fallback** using the ISDApp Community Controller pattern (one smartphone-owning champion per barangay broadcasting to feature phones) rather than requiring every fisher to install the app. **Integrate GCash and Maya from day one**; assume cash remains dominant for on-beach first-buy transactions but that palengke-layer settlements are digital-ready. **Localize in Ilocano first, Filipino second, English third**, with voice prompts and Tagalog/Ilocano species-name aliases (galunggong/tamodios, dilis/monamon). **Use household accounts** that allow husband-fisher and wife-tindera to share a login, reflecting the real unit of economic decision-making. **Design the species picker around the retail mix (bangus-tilapia-galunggong-dominant), not the catch mix** — because vendors price against what competing stalls sell, and bangus/tilapia/galunggong set the reference points. **Partner institutionally with BFAR RFO-1 Post-Harvest & Marketing Division** (Mea Baldonado) and LGU market supervisors (starting with Lorena Peridas at San Fernando City), because the 9 CFLCs and seven LGU markets are the only existing physical touchpoints that can absorb onboarding, weighing, and ice access. **Plan for Amihan/typhoon graceful degradation** — November through February and any named storm will take out data but not always SMS, and this is when fisher safety alerts and post-storm price spikes both matter most.

The deepest strategic insight, worth restating: **MERMAID's competitive moat is not price transparency — it is credit substitution with a human face.** Whoever figures out how to let a La Union fisher borrow ₱3,000 for fuel and ice on a Tuesday morning without signing away Thursday's catch will unlock the 10–30% price differential that currently accrues to compradors. That is a financial-services problem dressed as a marketplace problem, and it is the real design challenge behind every question this brief asked.
